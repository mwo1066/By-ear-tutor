# Vietnamese plurals — please check

We added four words and two rules to a beginner course. Everything below is a
claim we are making to a learner. **Cross out what is wrong, correct what is said
differently, tick what is fine.**

We are not Vietnamese speakers. These came from a dictionary and need a person.

---

## The rule we teach

> **Vietnamese has no plural pronouns. You put a word in front of a singular one.**
> `chúng` goes in front of a pronoun. `các` goes in front of an address word.

- [ ] Is that true as a general statement?

```
tôi  → chúng tôi        anh → các anh
nó   → chúng nó         bạn → các bạn
```

- [ ] Are `các anh` and `các bạn` how you actually say "you" to several people?
- [ ] Is there anything common we are missing — `bọn`, `tụi`, `mấy`?

## The four words, exactly as the tutor will say them

| word | what the tutor tells the learner it means |
| --- | --- |
| `chúng` | the word you put in front of a pronoun to make it plural |
| `chúng tôi` | we, **not** counting you |
| `chúng ta` | we, counting you |
| `họ` | they |

- [ ] `chúng tôi` — is "we, not counting the person I'm talking to" right?
- [ ] `chúng ta` — is "we, including the person I'm talking to" right?
- [ ] `họ` — is plain "they" right for a beginner? We tell them it does **not**
      change for men or women. True?
- [ ] `chúng` on its own — we do **not** teach it as a pronoun, because the
      dictionary says it is dismissive ("used for animals", "for people one holds
      in low regard"). Is that fair, or is it more ordinary than that?

## The second rule

> There is no neutral "we". `chúng ta đi` is an **invitation**. `chúng tôi đi` is
> an **announcement**. You have to pick.

- [ ] Is the invitation / announcement contrast right?
- [ ] Would a Vietnamese person actually notice if a foreigner picked wrong?

## What we deliberately left out

- [ ] `chúng nó` — judged too specific for a beginner. Agree?
- [ ] `chúng mình` — the dictionary calls it "familiar, respectful: me, you and
      our friends". Should a beginner have it instead of, or as well as,
      `chúng ta`?

## One thing we are unsure about

The dictionary marks `họ` as *"chiefly Northern Vietnam, only formal in other
dialects"*.

- [ ] In the south, is `họ` odd in ordinary speech? What would be used instead?
