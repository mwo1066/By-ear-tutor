# What is left to do: the words, then the sentences

Written 2026-08-23, the evening the frequency shelf was emptied. Meo: *"si on
focus que sur les mots puis les phrases, donne-moi une liste de trucs à faire."*

Where the course stands:

```
1278 words          the vocabulary, and it is done in quantity
  46 rules          all of them in the first 221 items
  22 sentences      1% of the course, and 8 of them hand-written
```

The order below is by dependency, not by importance. A sentence built on a wrong
gloss is wrong twice, so the words come first.

---

## THE WORDS

### 1. A Vietnamese speaker reads `GLOSSES-TO-VALIDATE.md`
**1346 items**, none of them read by anyone who speaks the language. The file is
in two parts, and part 1 matters more: the 221 hand-written items, in teaching
order, which a learner meets in their first hours and which the course drills
hardest. It also holds all 46 rules and all 22 sentences -- the frequency stock
has none of either. Part 2 is the 1125 bulk-glossed words by frequency. This is the
one task nothing else can substitute for, and it is cheap: reading a column and
marking what is wrong. See `OPEN.md` section 0 for what was caught while writing
them, which is the measure of what was missed.

**Blocks nothing, but everything downstream inherits its errors.**

### 2. Decide where the 1111 new words go — `changes/0030`
They currently arrive after item 221, in frequency order, because
`90_frequency_stock.toml` sorts last. Nobody chose that. Interleaved with the
curated lessons, or following them, is a content decision.

### 3. Fix `các`
The only startup warning left, and the only word in the course nobody has ruled
on. Either it gets a sayable gloss or it becomes a rule.

### 4. The cupboard — `changes/0029`
Country names and the like: known to the tutor, never queued. `Mỹ`, `Nhật Bản`,
`Pháp`, `Nga` and `Thái Lan` are in the queue today for want of it.

### 5. Reconsider the words shelved for an ENGLISH collision
Not errors — real distinctions English collapses, shelved under the duplicate
rule with that noted:

```
cơm / lúa       cooked rice / the plant        chữ / thư    a character / a letter
đầy / no        a full container / full fed    cũ / già     old things / old people
mặc / đeo       wear clothes / wear a watch    cứu / tiết kiệm  rescue / economise
nhẹ / ánh sáng  not heavy / illumination       thị trường / chợ  a market / a market
```

Each needs a gloss that carries the difference. Worth doing after the native
read, since she will find more of them.

## THE SENTENCES

### 6. Validate the 54 already written
`SENTENCES-TO-VALIDATE.md`, written when the course had 150 words. They use only
words taught at the time, so they are all still valid — but they are 54 against
1346 items.

### 7. Decide what a sentence is FOR at this size
The course has 22 constructions for 1278 words. That ratio is the question, not
a number to fix by adding sentences until it looks better. `METHOD.md` is the
place to look: the reference course spends most of its time on assembly, not on
vocabulary, and this course has inverted that in one evening.

### 8. Write sentences that use the new vocabulary
A construction declares its `pieces`, and every piece must be taught before it.
With 1278 words available the constraint is nearly gone — which makes it easy to
write sentences and easy to write bad ones. The 54 existing ones are the model
for what good looks like.

### 9. And the rules the sentences need
46 rules cover items 0-221 and nothing after. Sentences without the rules that
govern them are phrases to memorise, which is what this course exists not to do.
The six unwritten pronoun rules are the nearest example.
