# What the course would sound like

A simulation of about six minutes, as you would hear it. **Two voices:** the
tutor speaks the learner's language, Minh says only Vietnamese.

Nothing here is produced by the current code — this is what the "sentence as
vehicle" model would give. Read it as a learner, not as a developer.

---

**tutor** — So, *I* or *me*?

**you** — tôi

**tutor** — That's it. And again, the word for *to eat*?

**you** — ăn

**tutor** — Yes. And *rice*?

**you** — cơm

**tutor** — Good. Now put two of them together: *to eat rice*?

**you** — ăn cơm

**tutor** — Exactly.
**Minh** — ăn cơm.

**tutor** — And now the whole thing: *I eat rice*?

**you** — tôi ăn cơm

**tutor** — There it is.
**Minh** — Tôi ăn cơm.

---

*The first button. The sentence exists, now we bend it.*

**tutor** — You remember that `không` in front of the action negates it. So —
*I do not eat rice*?

**you** — tôi không ăn cơm

**tutor** — That's it.
**Minh** — Tôi không ăn cơm.

**tutor** — And to say it is already done, `rồi` goes at the end. *I have already
eaten*?

**you** — tôi ăn cơm rồi

**tutor** — Perfect.
**Minh** — Tôi ăn cơm rồi.

---

*A new word, two turns. Then it joins a sentence straight away.*

**tutor** — In Vietnamese, the word for *to drink* is **uống**.
**Minh** — uống. uống.

**tutor** — Your turn.

**you** — uống

**tutor** — Good. And again, *to drink*?

**you** — uống

**tutor** — Yes. Now, you already know *coffee* — `cà phê`. So *to drink
coffee*?

**you** — uống cà phê

**tutor** — Exactly.
**Minh** — uống cà phê.

**tutor** — And *I drink coffee*?

**you** — tôi uống cà phê

**tutor** — There it is.
**Minh** — Tôi uống cà phê.

---

*Same button, different sentence. The word comes back without being asked for on
its own.*

**tutor** — And if you do not drink any?

**you** — tôi không uống cà phê

**tutor** — That's it.
**Minh** — Tôi không uống cà phê.

**tutor** — Something else. `chưa` at the end turns the sentence into a question,
and it means *not yet*. Vietnamese people greet each other with it — *have you
eaten?*

**you** — anh ăn cơm chưa

**tutor** — Exactly. That is what you will be asked when you arrive somewhere.
**Minh** — Anh ăn cơm chưa?

---

*An adjective comes in. It needs no "là" — the rule arrives after the use.*

**tutor** — The word for *good*, about taste, is **ngon**.
**Minh** — ngon. ngon.

**tutor** — Your turn.

**you** — ngon

**tutor** — Good. So, *the coffee is good* — and careful, there is **no** verb
*to be* here.

**you** — cà phê ngon

**tutor** — Exactly. You have just done something: in front of a describing word,
the `là` disappears. You say `cà phê ngon`, never `cà phê là ngon`.
**Minh** — Cà phê ngon.

**tutor** — And to say *very good*? `rất` goes in front.

**you** — cà phê rất ngon

**tutor** — There it is.
**Minh** — Cà phê rất ngon.

---

*Seven minutes later, in the same lesson. `ăn` and `cơm` come back — in a
sentence we have never said.*

**tutor** — And the rice, how was it?

**you** — cơm ngon

**tutor** — Yes. And *the rice is very good*?

**you** — cơm rất ngon

**tutor** — Perfect.
**Minh** — Cơm rất ngon.

**tutor** — One last thing. To ask whether something is good, you wrap it: `có`
in front, `không` at the end. *Is the rice good?*

**you** — cơm có ngon không

**tutor** — That's it.
**Minh** — Cơm có ngon không?

---

## What you have just heard, mechanically

| | |
| --- | --- |
| words taught | `tôi` `ăn` `cơm` `uống` `cà phê` `ngon` — **6** |
| sentences produced by you | **17** |
| times `ăn` passed through your mouth | 5 |
| times `cơm` did | 6 |
| seeds actually written in the content | **3** — `ăn cơm`, `uống cà phê`, `cà phê ngon` |

The other fourteen sentences are variations: the past, negation, `rồi`, `chưa`,
`rất`, the `có…không` question. **None of them was written by hand.**

And two rules were taught without ever being announced in advance — the `là` that
disappears, and the `có…không` wrapping — because they arrived **after** you had
produced them.
